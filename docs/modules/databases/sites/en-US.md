Open an interface and choose {{ui:sites.publish}}.  You need permission to manage database sharing.  Give the site a {{ui:sites.title}} and {{ui:sites.address}}, select its pages, and drag their named rows under {{ui:sites.pages}} to put them in order.  With a move control focused, Space picks up the page, Up or Down moves it, Space drops, and Escape cancels.  Alt with Up or Down moves a page immediately.  Mouse dragging starts after a small movement.  On touch screens and with a pen, press and hold before moving.  Each page has its own title, address, description, and optional field for record addresses.

Choose a theme and color scheme.  The header shows the site's title or the image you choose under {{ui:sites.logo}}.  Its navigation follows the order of the published pages and marks the current page.  Set a page's {{ui:sites.navLabel}} to change its navigation text while keeping its page heading and document title.  Leave it blank to use the page title.  Turn off {{ui:sites.showInNav}} to leave a published page out of both the desktop navigation and the phone menu.  The page still opens at its address and through buttons and links.  On a phone, {{ui:sites.menu}} opens the navigation without JavaScript.

Choose {{ui:sites.addHeaderButton}} under {{ui:sites.headerButton}} to add one primary button beside the navigation.  Give it a {{ui:interfaces.button.label}} and choose {{ui:interfaces.button.url}} or {{ui:interfaces.button.page}} for its destination.  A page destination links only while that page is published, including pages hidden from navigation.  On a phone, the button stays visible beside {{ui:sites.menu}}.  Choose {{ui:interfaces.hero.removeButton}} to remove it.

{{ui:sites.footer}} adds text at the bottom of every page, up to 500 characters.  Existing footers keep their plain text formatting until you edit this field.  Saving other settings keeps the footer's current format.  Editing the footer enables the same Markdown subset as paragraphs: emphasis, links, line breaks, and lists.  For example, `[About](about)` links to a published page, and `[Partner](https://example.com)` links to an outside address.  HTML remains text, and unsafe addresses never become links.  {{ui:sites.favicon}}, {{ui:sites.logo}}, and {{ui:sites.socialImage}} select images already attached to records.  The image must remain readable to the publisher.  A verified custom domain enabled for sites can serve one site at its root.  Only the owner or an administrator with the Integrations role chooses a {{ui:sites.domain}}, since the domain then serves the site in place of its other public pages.  Anyone else who publishes the site again keeps the domain it has.

Choose {{ui:sites.save}} to publish.  Anyone with the address can read the site without signing in.  Records paginate, filter controls narrow their lists, and record details have their own addresses.  Numbers and charts read through the publisher's permissions.  Form buttons open public forms and use the same submission checks as other public forms.  Publishing also shares those forms.  A button that opens a web address becomes a link styled as a button.  {{ui:interfaces.button.page}} selects another page in the same interface.  It links to that page only while the destination is also published.  Links within the site open in the same tab.  Links that leave the site carry protections for the page they open.

Blocks follow the interface page's grid.  Blocks placed side by side stay beside each other on wider screens and stack in reading order on a phone.  Choose {{ui:interfaces.records.cards}} for a responsive grid of record cards.  {{ui:interfaces.records.cover}} selects one attachment field for their covers.  Only clean images and videos from that field appear, and the publisher must still be able to read each record and export the field.  Choosing a cover publishes that image field for covers without adding its other values to the card.  Turn off {{ui:interfaces.records.showFieldNames}} for lists and cards to show values only, with the first selected field as each record's heading, while table column headings stay visible.

A text block can be a heading, a paragraph, or a {{ui:interfaces.text.lead}} for a larger opening paragraph.  All three styles accept a small Markdown subset: blank lines start paragraphs, line breaks stay line breaks, `*text*` adds emphasis, `**text**` adds strong emphasis, and `[text](https://example.com)` makes a link.  In the interface designer, saved paragraphs also retain Markdown headings, inline code, and safe email links.  In the heading style, each paragraph becomes a heading, and lists stay lists.  Begin list items with `-` or `1.` for bulleted or numbered lists.  HTML remains text, and unsafe addresses such as `javascript:` and `data:` never become links.  Relative links may name another published page, such as `[About](about)`.

Add a {{ui:interfaces.block.hero}} for a page opening with a heading, a lead paragraph, up to two buttons, and an optional record image or video.  Add {{ui:interfaces.block.media}} to show one image or video from a record's attachment field.  Set {{ui:interfaces.media.alt}} to describe the image or video, and add a {{ui:interfaces.media.caption}} when it needs context.  By default, a video uses the browser's player with controls and does not autoplay.  An image beside the video in the same attachment field supplies its poster.  The designer follows attachment edits and poster changes as they happen.  Removing an attachment or trashing its record hides the media while preserving the block's saved selection.  Supported images are PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF, and icons up to five megabytes.  Supported videos are MP4, WebM, and Ogg.  Media stays on the site's origin, and video playback can request only the bytes it needs.

Bar, column, line, and donut charts render as charts using the site's theme colors.  Their data tables remain available to screen readers and appear when printing.  Lines leave gaps for missing values.  Bars and columns show negative values on the other side of zero.  Donut slices show positive values, with zero, negative, and missing values retained in the table.  Other chart types keep their visible tables.

The publisher dialog's {{ui:sites.preview}} shows the selected page with your draft settings before publication.  Choose {{ui:sites.previewUpdate}} after edits, and switch between phone and desktop widths.  Preview media uses your current access.  Every new block and setting is available in the interface designer and the page JSON editor.

Only fields explicitly selected in record blocks or selected for images, videos, or card covers are published.  An empty field selection publishes no fields.  The primary field is included only if selected.  A record detail page can show only records in its source block's filters.  Address filters and sorts cannot request hidden fields.  Lookups show names, as view links do: choices, people, linked records, and files, never their stored IDs.

Text in {{ui:sites.title}}, page descriptions, and records stays text, including HTML tags and script snippets.  Publishing does not execute that content.  Publishing a site never grants access to another organization's records.  Selected records must belong to the publisher's organization and pass its sharing permissions.

A field restricted from anyone, and anything computed from one, is never published, even when the publisher can see it, just as a view link never shares it.  A records block leaves such a field out.  A records block, number, or chart that filters, sorts, groups, or sums by one, directly or through its view, shows nothing until it no longer does, and an image in such a field isn't shown.  This applies when the field is restricted or changed later, without publishing again.

Publishing saves a snapshot of the selected interface pages.  Editing the interface does not change that snapshot until you publish again.  Record values remain current.  Saving again makes you the publisher.  If the publisher loses database access or leaves the organization, the site stops working until an authorized person publishes it again.  Field and record permission changes apply immediately.  A records block follows its view's filter and order as they are now, read as the publisher, so someone who can't see every field and record in the database can't change the settings of a view that another person's site reads.  Nor can they change the type or options of a field the site shows, sorts, or filters by, or of one such a field is worked out from, restore one from the trash, or change a named function its formulas call.  A site is a public link, so when the publisher's unit or organization turns public links off, publishing is refused and the site answers as gone, like other paused links, until public links are on again.

{{ui:sites.unpublish}} takes the site offline immediately, releases its custom domain, and keeps its address reserved.  Republishing uses the same address.  Public forms shared by publishing remain independently shared until you stop sharing them from their form settings.

Pages include server-rendered content, canonical addresses, social previews, a sitemap, and a robots file.  {{ui:sites.noIndex}} asks search engines not to index the site.  This is a request to search engines, not an access restriction.

Set {{ui:interfaces.records.pageSize}} next to {{ui:interfaces.records.display}} to choose a whole number from 1 to 100 records per page.  The default is 25.  The setting applies to every records display, and the designer's preview follows it.  Each block pages independently with {{ui:sites.previous}} and {{ui:sites.next}}, up to 10,000 records in.  Sites published before this setting keep 25 records per page until you change the setting and publish again.  Pages are limited to one megabyte.  Browsers and proxies never cache site responses.  The site uses no cookies.  Content, navigation, links, and native media controls work with scripts off.  Styles support dark schemes, reduced motion, keyboard focus, and print, and fonts and scripts load only from the site's origin.

Each server process keeps rendered pages for up to five minutes, and serves one again only while nothing in the database has changed since.  The publisher's access is checked on every request, cached or not, so a change to it applies at once.  Each network address may make 240 requests a minute to sites, with an IPv6 address counted by its /64.  Each site renders at most 1,200 new pages a minute on each server process.  Only pages that aren't cached count toward that, and it's well above what one address may ask for, so one visitor can't use it up for everyone else.  Past either limit, the site answers 429 Too Many Requests with a Retry-After header.

Search engines get what every visitor gets: the same server-rendered pages, the sitemap of the site's pages, and record pages through the links on them.  A crawler counts as one visitor per address, and one that asks faster than an address may is told to wait with a Retry-After header, which search engines honor.

Forms on a site show conditional questions only while an earlier visible answer matches their condition.  Hidden questions are neither required nor submitted.  Currency answers have an amount and currency code.  Line items use the configured columns, and {{ui:lineItems.add}} adds another line.  Blank lines are left out.  A required line-item question needs at least one populated line.  After submission, the form shows its confirmation message, or the default confirmation if no custom message is set.

Forms on a site take the same checks as other public forms, including the hourly limit per address your server's operator sets.  A form that takes payment goes on to Stripe Checkout when it's submitted, and only its page allows that.

## Bands, disclosure, and record sections

Set {{ui:interfaces.band}} on text, hero, image or video, records, number, chart, and button blocks.  {{ui:interfaces.band.none}} keeps the existing background.  {{ui:interfaces.band.quiet}}, {{ui:interfaces.band.accent}}, and {{ui:interfaces.band.inverse}} use the theme's accessible colors.  Consecutive rows with the same band join into one full-width band on the published page, with content at the usual page width.  A row with different bands uses no band.  A block spanning rows joins those rows when deciding the band.  The designer previews the selected colors.

Choose {{ui:interfaces.records.accordion}} under {{ui:interfaces.records.display}} to make each record a disclosure.  The first selected field is the question, and the remaining fields are the answer.  {{ui:interfaces.records.showFieldNames}} controls their field names.  Tab reaches a question, and Enter or Space opens it.  Disclosures work with scripts off and print with their answers visible.

Choose {{ui:interfaces.records.sections}} to turn records into page sections.  The first selected field supplies the heading, and the remaining fields supply paragraphs.  Headings and paragraphs preserve line breaks.  {{ui:interfaces.records.showFieldNames}} controls their field names.  {{ui:interfaces.records.sectionCover}} supplies an image or video from an attachment field, and {{ui:interfaces.records.altField}} supplies its description.  {{ui:interfaces.records.linkField}} supplies a web address, and {{ui:interfaces.records.linkLabelField}} supplies its button label.  Empty or unsafe addresses show no link.  Words and media alternate sides on wide screens, and words come first on phones.  The records keep the view's filter, order, and paging.  Description and button-label fields show people and linked records by name, just as selected fields do.

## Looping clips and enlarged pictures

Choose {{ui:sites.videoFile}} under {{ui:interfaces.media.type}} for a hero or image or video block.  {{ui:interfaces.media.playback}} defaults to {{ui:interfaces.media.controls}}.  {{ui:interfaces.media.loop}} plays a muted, inline loop without controls.  It also applies to videos in record sections, cards, and lists.  Readers see a still poster with controls to start playback when they request reduced motion or the site uses {{ui:sites.motion.none}} under {{ui:sites.motion}}.  Looping clips always stay muted.  With scripts off, the native controls remain available.  Published heroes, looping clips, and section videos reserve a widescreen frame when neither dimensions nor a poster are available, so loading the clip keeps the following content in place.

Turn on {{ui:interfaces.media.enlarge}} for an image or video block, or beside {{ui:interfaces.records.sectionCover}}, {{ui:interfaces.records.cover}}, or {{ui:interfaces.records.listCover}} for record sections, cards, or lists.  Press the picture or clip to open it at full size, use Escape, {{ui:interfaces.media.enlarge.close}}, or {{ui:interfaces.media.enlarge.closeVideo}} to close it, and focus returns to the picture or clip.  A large looping clip keeps playing silently.  The control is labeled {{ui:interfaces.media.enlarge.open}} or {{ui:interfaces.media.enlarge.video}} for screen readers.  With scripts off, its link opens the original file.

## Subtle motion

{{ui:sites.motion}} defaults to {{ui:sites.motion.subtle}}.  Blocks below the first screen fade in and rise slightly once as they come into view, while the first screen stays still.  Following an in-page link, focusing a control, or using find makes the passage visible immediately.  The header logo fades in only on the first page of a visit.  Pictures and looping clips open and close with a short fade and scale, and supported browsers cross-fade between pages while keeping the header steady.  Buttons, navigation links, cards, and folding rows respond briefly to hover, focus, and press.  Folding answers fade open and closed where the browser supports it.

Choose {{ui:sites.motion.none}} to turn off these effects and automatic loops.  A visitor's reduced-motion preference always takes priority.  With scripts off, or while they are loading, every block and the logo are visible.  Motion changes opacity and transforms without changing the layout.  Print shows every block and folding answer.
